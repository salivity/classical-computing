Why Does Multi-Bit Addition Require a Full Adder?

Chaining multiple bits together for binary addition in classical computing requires a full adder because each bit position beyond the least significant bit must account for three inputs: the two corresponding bits from the numbers being added and a carry-in bit from the previous position. While a half adder can add two single bits, it lacks a third input for this carry-in bit, making it impossible to propagate carries across a multi-bit ripple-carry adder.

The Limitation of a Half Adder

A half adder is a combinational logic circuit designed to add two single-bit binary numbers, \(A\) and \(B\). It produces two outputs: a Sum (\(S\)) and a Carry-out (\(C_{out}\)). The boolean expressions for a half adder are:

\[S = A \oplus B\] \[C_{out} = A \cdot B\]

This works for the least significant bit (LSB) of a multi-bit addition, where there is no previous column to generate a carry. However, if \(1 + 1 = 10_2\) occurs at the LSB, the \(0\) is recorded as the sum for that column, and the \(1\) must be carried over to the next most significant bit position.

How a Full Adder Solves Carry Propagation

To continue adding at the next bit position, the circuit must evaluate three binary inputs: \(A\), \(B\), and the carry-in (\(C_{in}\)) from the preceding stage. A full adder is built specifically to handle these three inputs, producing a Sum (\(S\)) and a new Carry-out (\(C_{out}\)):

\[S = A \oplus B \oplus C_{in}\] \[C_{out} = (A \cdot B) + (C_{in} \cdot (A \oplus B))\]

By incorporating \(C_{in}\), the full adder ensures that any overflow from a lower bit position is accurately included in the current column's calculation and, if necessary, propagated further to the next bit position.

Chaining Multi-Bit Addition

In classical computing architectures, multi-bit numbers are added by chaining adders together in a structure known as a ripple-carry adder. For an \(n\)-bit addition, the \(C_{out}\) of bit position \(i\) is wired directly to the \(C_{in}\) of bit position \(i+1\). Without the carry-in input provided by a full adder, this chain cannot be formed, and multi-bit binary arithmetic would fail whenever a carry is generated.
