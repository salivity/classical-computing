Why Is RISC-V Gaining Adoption for Custom Silicon?

The open-standard RISC-V modular instruction set architecture is rapidly gaining traction in classical computing because it eliminates licensing fees, removes vendor lock-in, and allows engineers to tailor processor designs to specific workloads. Unlike proprietary architectures such as x86 or Arm, RISC-V provides a free, extensible foundation that organizations can inspect, modify, and manufacture without restrictive commercial agreements.

Freedom from Licensing Fees and Royalties

Traditional processor architectures require upfront licensing fees and per-unit royalties that can significantly increase the cost of custom silicon. RISC-V is governed by RISC-V International as an open standard, meaning any company, university, or individual can implement the instruction set without paying royalties. This economic model lowers the barrier to entry for startups, hyperscalers, and embedded device makers developing specialized chips.

Modularity and Custom Extensions

A defining feature of RISC-V is its modular design. Rather than forcing every chip to carry decades of legacy instructions, RISC-V starts with a small, stable base integer instruction set and allows designers to add only the standard extensions they need, such as:

  • M for integer multiplication and division
  • A for atomic operations
  • F and D for single- and double-precision floating-point arithmetic
  • V for vector processing

Beyond standard extensions, RISC-V reserves opcode space for custom instructions. Hardware teams can build domain-specific accelerators for cryptography, signal processing, or machine learning inference directly into the processor pipeline, improving performance per watt compared to general-purpose cores.

Supply Chain Independence and Transparency

Geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions have made architectural independence a strategic priority. Because RISC-V is not controlled by a single corporation or nation, organizations can design and source silicon from multiple foundries and IP vendors. In addition, the open nature of the specification enables thorough security auditing, which is particularly valuable for government, defense, and critical infrastructure applications.

Growing Software and Tooling Ecosystem

Early skepticism around RISC-V often centered on software maturity, but the ecosystem has expanded substantially. Major operating systems, including Linux, FreeBSD, and Android, support RISC-V, alongside mainstream compilers such as GCC and LLVM. Hardware verification tools, open-source core implementations, and commercial IP offerings have also matured, reducing the engineering effort required to bring custom RISC-V silicon from concept to production.

Conclusion

RISC-V is gaining adoption in custom silicon because its open, royalty-free, and modular architecture aligns with the modern computing industry's shift toward specialized hardware. By combining cost efficiency, design flexibility, and supply chain independence, RISC-V has established itself as a practical alternative to proprietary instruction sets across embedded systems, data centers, and classical computing workloads.
